# Vunesp traps and how to take the exam

## The traps that show up the most

1. Options with absolute words ("always", "never", "totally", "exclusively", "any"). They are almost always wrong.
2. A true statement that doesn't answer the question, or that the text doesn't say. This is the most common distractor.
3. A concept with the author swapped: Lamarck and Darwin, Thomson and Rutherford, latitude and longitude, Hobbes and Locke, myth and religion.
4. Units: km/h to m/s, mg to g, mL to L, J to kJ, MW to W, hectares to m².
5. A condition hidden in the question: "above R$ 100", "at the same time of day", "together with governments", "ignoring friction".
6. A wrong answer sitting halfway through the calculation. In the 2024 hydroelectric plant question, 72 (MW per gate) was one of the options, but the question asked for the flow rate.
7. Mixing up where the maximum happens (x of the vertex) with how much the maximum is (y of the vertex).
8. Interest calculated on the full price, when it should be on the amount that was financed.

## In human sciences, the answer is in the text

In history, geography and philosophy, the right answer is almost always a paraphrase of the excerpt in the question. If an option contradicts the text, it's out right away. When in doubt, I go back to the text before choosing.

## Method during the exam

The exam gives 4 hours per booklet. At a normal pace one booklet takes about 1h30, so there is time left to check.

1. First pass at a normal pace. I mark each question with `!` (sure), `?` (don't know the content) or `-` (there is a word I don't know).
2. Second pass on every `!` and `?`. I read the other 4 options and check against the text. A wrong "sure" is dangerous because you never go back to it.
3. I redo every calculation from scratch without looking at my first try.
4. I never leave a question blank. The score is just the number of right answers, so I guess on whatever is left.

## How I practice with past exams

- I take each booklet with a timer and write my answers on paper, like an answer sheet.
- In the PDFs I use, the answer key is on the last page, so I don't scroll to the end before I finish.
- After grading, I split my mistakes into three kinds: didn't know the content, didn't understand the question, or a careless slip. Each kind needs a different fix.
