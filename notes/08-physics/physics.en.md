# Physics (1st year)

On the exam, physics is almost always a two or three step calculation inside a story (hydroelectric plant, electric car, tower crane, race drivers). Almost all of it comes out of these formulas.

| Formula | When to use it | Exam example |
|---|---|---|
| v = d ÷ t | average speed, travel time | canoe doing 1,000 m in 3min45s (convert everything to seconds) |
| a = Δv ÷ t | how much the speed changes per second | drivers at Interlagos: t = Δv ÷ a for each one |
| d = (v₀ + v) ÷ 2 × t | distance while speeding up or braking | car braking until it stops |
| F = m · a | net force | rope lifting a sofa: T = m(g + a) |
| F = k · x | spring force (k = F ÷ x) | graph with three springs |
| Ek = m · v² ÷ 2 | energy of motion | car going from 0 to 108 km/h |
| Ep = m · g · h | energy of height, work done by weight | aqueduct, tower crane, water at a dam |
| P = energy ÷ time | power (W = J/s) | dam and electric car |

## Energy

- Ek (kinetic) is about motion. Speed is squared, so doubling the speed makes four times the energy.
- Ep (gravitational potential) is about height. You just multiply mass, gravity (10 m/s² on the exam) and height.
- Without friction, what is lost in height is gained in speed: mechanical energy (Ep + Ek) stays the same.

### The power routine

1. Convert everything to SI (km/h to m/s, minutes to seconds).
2. Check if it's about motion (Ek) or height (Ep).
3. Calculate the energy.
4. Divide by the time.

A 1,200 kg electric car going from 0 to 30 m/s in 15 s: Ek = 1,200 × 900 ÷ 2 = 540,000 J, P = 540,000 ÷ 15 = 36,000 W.

A 1,000 kg car going from 0 to 36 km/h in 5 s: 36 km/h = 10 m/s, Ek = 1,000 × 100 ÷ 2 = 50,000 J, P = 50,000 ÷ 5 = 10,000 W = 10 kW. The 36 in the statement is just the starting data.

Water falling 20 m at 50 kg per second: the energy per second is 50 × 10 × 20 = 10,000 J, so the power is 10,000 W = 10 kW.

A 216 MW dam with 3 gates and a 78 m drop: each gate has 72 MW. 72,000,000 = m × 10 × 78, so m ≈ 92,300 kg per second, which is about 92 m³/s. The 72 was a wrong option.

### Hanging block on a spring

At rest, the spring force is equal to the weight. So it takes two steps: first the weight, P = m · g, then F = k · x to find the stretch. A 2 kg block on a spring with k = 400 N/m: P = 2 × 10 = 20 N, x = 20 ÷ 400 = 0.05 m = 5 cm. The 20 is the force, not the stretch, and it shows up as a wrong option.

## Motion

- Displacement is final position minus initial position. It's zero if you came back to the same place. Distance is everything you traveled.
- On a v × t graph, the displacement is the area under the line.
- Higher speed doesn't mean arriving first: in the race driver question, the one who reached the point faster got there later.

## Newton

- 1st law (inertia): when the bus brakes, your body keeps going forward.
- 2nd law: F = m · a.
- 3rd law (action and reaction): a swimmer pushes the wall and the wall pushes back.

## Projectiles and falling

Without air resistance, mass changes nothing. To make a horizontal launch go farther, increase the height (more time falling) or the speed.

## Gravitation and Kepler

- The smaller the distance, the bigger the gravitational force.
- At perihelion (closest to the Sun) a planet moves faster.
- Seasons come from Earth's tilt, not from the distance to the Sun.

## Conversions that cost points

- km/h ÷ 3.6 = m/s
- minutes × 60 = seconds
- 1 kW = 1,000 W, 1 MW = 1,000,000 W, 1 kJ = 1,000 J, 1 W = 1 J/s
- k · x² ÷ 2 is the spring's energy. To find k, I use F = k · x.

Before any calculation I compare the unit the question asks for with the unit of the data. If they don't match, I convert first. A car going from 0 to 72 km/h in 8 s: 72 km/h = 20 m/s, so a = 20 ÷ 8 = 2.5 m/s². Using 72 directly gives 9, which is a wrong option.

When there are several conversions in a row, I do them one at a time. A 2 MW plant running for 30 minutes: 2 MW = 2,000,000 W, 30 min = 1,800 s, energy = 2,000,000 × 1,800 = 3.6 × 10⁹ J. If the answer is in kJ, I divide by 1,000 and get 3.6 × 10⁶ kJ.
