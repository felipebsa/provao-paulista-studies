# Philosophy (1st year)

The mistakes I made most here were about names, not interpretation. So this note is basically a table to memorize.

## Names and ideas

| Name or concept | The idea in one line |
|---|---|
| Socrates | "I only know that I know nothing". The method of asking questions until the person sees they didn't know is dialectic (or maieutics) |
| Descartes | rationalism: knowledge comes from reason. Methodical doubt, "I think, therefore I am" |
| Locke (knowledge) | empiricism: the mind starts as a blank slate and everything comes from experience |
| Hobbes | the state of nature is a war of all against all, so you need an absolute sovereign (Leviathan) |
| Locke (politics) | natural rights (life, liberty, property) and a limited government to protect them |
| Rousseau | people are born good and society corrupts them. The contract is based on the general will |
| Montesquieu | separation of the three powers |
| Common sense | inherited, without method, fragmented. It's not "always false" |
| Myth → logos | philosophy starts by taking the gods out of the explanation of nature |
| Philosophical reflection | thought turning back on itself to understand reality |
| Bioethics | ethics applied to life, medicine and science. Focus on protecting the individual |
| Hans Jonas | imperative of responsibility: think about the consequences of our actions for future generations |
| Lévinas | alterity: recognizing and respecting the other as different from me |
| Achille Mbembe | necropolitics: the State deciding who is "disposable" (closing borders to refugees, for example) |
| Duchamp (readymade) | an everyday object becomes art and loses its practical function |

## The three social contract thinkers

All three talk about the social contract. What changes is how each one sees the state of nature:

- Hobbes: without government there is war, so the State has to be strong and absolute.
- Locke: people already have natural rights, and government exists to protect them, but it is limited.
- Rousseau: people are born good, and it's society that corrupts them.

## Empiricism and rationalism

Empiricism comes from the Greek word for experience. So empiricism says knowledge comes from the senses (Locke), and rationalism says it comes from reason (Descartes).

## The philosophy trap

The exam likes to swap definitions between options. In 2024, the "mythical" option described religion and the "religious" one described myth. I check each option against the table before marking.

Almost every question comes with an excerpt from an author. If I read it carefully, the answer is usually there, even without remembering the name.
